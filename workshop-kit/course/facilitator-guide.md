# Facilitator guide

How to run the five sessions. Read `README.md` first. This file says what to bring, how the
room runs, how to use the story slots and the worked example, and how the timings add up.

## What the facilitator brings

- The five session files, printed or on a second screen. The fiche of the day, one copy per
  attendee.
- The activity sheets of the day, one copy per attendee. The sheets are in `activities/`
  and each session's part 3 names which ones.
- The example folder, `example/messy/` and `example/ordered/`, as a zip each attendee can
  unpack, and on the facilitator's machine for the demonstrations.
- A tool that reads a folder, set to ask before it edits or runs anything. The permission
  mode and the file names are the only tool-specific steps in the course. Each one carries
  a "checked on" date in the session file. When the tool's own documentation disagrees with
  the session file, the documentation wins and the session file is fixed.
- A whiteboard or large paper. Sessions 1, 3 and 4 draw on it.

## What the attendee brings

- One real recurring job, chosen before session 1. The job ends with a person receiving
  something. The attendee brings three real inputs for it, with any private detail removed.
- A laptop. An assistant account that can attach files. From session 3, a tool that reads a
  folder.
- From session 4, a copy of the folder where that job's files live today. A copy, never the
  original.

## How a session runs

Each session keeps the same shape:

1. Arrival and the recap of the last fiche.
2. The concepts block. The facilitator teaches from a thing in the room, traces what is
   beneath it, and ends with the three-line exercise. Attendees write the three lines.
3. Activities, in pairs where the sheet says so. The facilitator walks the room with the
   check from the session file: what success looks like, what the common failure looks like.
4. The break, 15 minutes, after the first two activities.
5. The story slot. The facilitator tells one story from their own practice. The slot in the
   session file says what the story must carry. It never says the story.
6. The closing exercise and the fiche.

## The story slots

Each session has one marked slot. The slot names the point the story must land, for example
"a wrong fact caught late and what it cost". The facilitator chooses the story. A story that
names a real client, a real price or a real contact is told without them. The slot has a
time. A story that runs long takes its time from the recap, never from an activity.

## The worked example

The example business is fictional and simple on purpose. Its folder is small enough to read
in ten minutes and messy enough to break in the six ways the course needs. `example/README.md`
lists each file by its role. `example/scenarios.md` gives each scenario as a card: the setup,
what the facilitator does, what the room should see, and the session that uses it.

The messy folder is never fixed in place. Each attendee works on their own unpacked copy.
Between sessions the facilitator resets to the zip.

## The attendee's own folder

From session 4 the attendee builds the folder for their own job. Session 5 runs the walk test
on it. An attendee who has no job of their own works on the example folder and runs the walk
test on `example/ordered/`. The test passes there. That is the point of the ordered version.

## Checks during activities

Each activity in a session file ends with two lines:

- **Worked:** what the facilitator sees on the screen or hears from the pair.
- **Common failure:** what the facilitator sees when it did not work, and the one question
  that moves the pair on.

The facilitator reads these before the activity, not during it.

## Timings

Each session is 210 minutes. The block timings are in part 5 of each session file and sum to
210 there. The five sessions sum to 1050 minutes, 17 h 30.

| Session | Concepts | Activities | Break | Story | Close | Arrival and recap | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 45 | 95 | 15 | 15 | 25 | 15 | 210 |
| 2 | 40 | 105 | 15 | 15 | 20 | 15 | 210 |
| 3 | 45 | 100 | 15 | 15 | 20 | 15 | 210 |
| 4 | 40 | 110 | 15 | 15 | 15 | 15 | 210 |
| 5 | 40 | 115 | 15 | 10 | 15 | 15 | 210 |
| Total | 210 | 525 | 75 | 70 | 95 | 75 | 1050 |

"Close" covers the closing exercise, the fiche and the homework line.

## Rules the facilitator keeps

- Read a gate, never tick one. The attendee ticks their own.
- Never fix the attendee's folder for them. Ask the question in the check instead.
- Every historical or numerical claim in the concepts block carries its hedge. Keep the
  hedge when you say it aloud.
- Tool steps carry a date. Say the date when you show the step.
